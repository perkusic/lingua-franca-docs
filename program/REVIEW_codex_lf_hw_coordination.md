## Task 4 Technical findings and revisions

**High — Figure 5, authorization omitted.** Original: “TAG_B = min(h0, h) (final, input closure holds).” An admitted frame and B's NET give B work, not a bound on upstream future sends. Counterexample with scalar tags and positive delays: A executes at 10, sends over a delay-10 connection for B at 20, and completes 10. A still has work at 11 on a delay-1 connection that can send to B at 12. With B's timer at 25, the original rule grants through 20 although 12 is still possible. On a single fixed-delay edge, a separately proved scalar-time completion contract may exclude such sends, but the diagram did not establish the general rule, and the reference computes strict delayed bounds. Also, if h0 < h the original B execution at h does not follow from its grant. **Replacement:** Figure 5 picks h < h0, publishes admission and NET coherently, records A completion and next event, then evaluates delayed completion or a strict earliest future arrival before any final permission covering h. See reactor-c [`core/federated/RTI/rti_common.c` L89–L129, L165–L288](https://github.com/lf-lang/reactor-c/blob/ccd05124e4b2086de045a6857702ae5636da30a6/core/federated/RTI/rti_common.c#L89-L288). A further NET is useful in this trace, not a universal prerequisite when completion already suffices.

**High — In-transit retirement and visibility conflated.** Original: “the data path's admission acknowledgement clears it.” [`core/federated/RTI/rti_remote.c` L543–L549 and L728–L766](https://github.com/lf-lang/reactor-c/blob/ccd05124e4b2086de045a6857702ae5636da30a6/core/federated/RTI/rti_remote.c#L728-L766) records a forwarded tag if above destination completion, deduplicates by tag, clamps effective NET on updates and removes through receiver LTC. Ordered forwarding and later grants make data available; the pending set is **not** a barrier requiring receiver completion before a grant. Admission is earlier, so releasing that bound alone is weaker: an admitted but unpublished event at h can disappear from effective NET, affecting downstream bounds. Early retirement would require an atomic equivalent representation of pending work and a refinement proof. **Replacement:** preserve pending tags until receiver LTC; clear a separate proposed visibility obligation on ordered admission, with no gap in the receiver's NET.

**High — Clamp does not prove safety and liveness together.** Original: “presents `min(TAG, NET)` to the unchanged scheduler … so the assertion … is never triggered.” If G=15 and local NET=20, the clamp is 15, not a pending event. If the presence bit corresponds to an external event at 18, presenting that bit at 15 can mis-tag it. With external-only work and invalid core NET, the unchanged scheduler cannot take its external branch. With valid local NET changing between idle and firing, the combinational selection can change under backpressure. Also, an authorization bound from [`core/tag.c` L159–L177](https://github.com/lf-lang/reactor-c/blob/ccd05124e4b2086de045a6857702ae5636da30a6/core/tag.c#L159-L177) can have microstep UINT_MAX although the executable candidate has microstep zero. **Replacement:** hold a full-width ceiling; present only a stable real candidate and matching closed snapshot, and rescan after consumption. By construction g ≤ G, and no narrowing can enlarge G. Liveness still needs eventual ready, progress and a valid external-only path; prove those at G-A1 or change the scheduler under OQ-8. T-A2 now distinguishes local core NET from the participant's reported NET.

**High — Egress acceptance does not prove completion visibility.** Original: “every output frame for g has been accepted by the data path and its MSG_SENT has been sent.” FR-19 lines 599 and 990–1013 require visibility of outputs through g before LTC; arbitrary FIFO enqueue and unacknowledged control send are insufficient. With no output, OR of input readiness is not a proof of all local work finished. **Replacement:** wait for recorded send and receiver-visible output commitment before publishing upstream progress that depends on it; certify all work at the tag, even when no outputs exist. No requirement to wait for receiver execution/LTC to release the sender. T-A4 must stall actual data visibility, exercise multi-cycle reactions/DMA and include a no-output transaction.

**High — Scalar tardy execution cannot implement a microstep retry.** Original: “invoke a hardware tardy handler with the intended tag.” If this means executing at (t,m+1) on the unchanged scalar reactor, the encoding either aliases t or rejects the microstep. **Replacement:** initial B-HW2 handles drop/count or fault out of band, preserving intended tag in diagnostics. A semantic microstep retry needs a widened tag, queue, scheduler and completion contract. Detect arrivals against the committed frontier, including the currently executing tag, not only completed work.

**High — Figure 4 arrival and timeout ordering.** Original: “Input version changed since the check?” looped to the input test after candidate selection, and tardiness was tested only after execute. New earlier ingress can change the candidate; a check followed by unprotected commit still races. Reference: choose from event and system queues, cap at stop, wait for physical time unless bypassed, acquire over connected inputs, use maximum unresolved wait and retry on notification. It has per-input locks, not the proposed atomic recheck. **Replacement:** for the narrower hardware experiment, select from local/admitted work, reselect on wake or changed version, require atomic snapshot commit, check the maximum applicable deadline and classify arrivals independently. Explicit absence/progress, treating disconnect as fault and refusing unknown-input commits in B-HW1 are intentional differences, not reactor-uc behavior.

**Medium — Timing inequality needs a precise endpoint and tie rule.** Original: “D_i(g) ≥ B_i(g) under a stated boundary rule.” The distinction from `max_wait` is correct. Equality is safe only if admission at the bound precedes commit; otherwise require strict separation. Define B at receiver-visible admission, including queues and sampling error. The runtime uses g.time + max positive wait over unresolved connected inputs at each scan, not an inferred worst-case bound. No numerical board bound is claimed.

**High — A trace oracle is not an A53 board coordinator.** Original: “the verified policy for the first milestone is … running on the Arm cores.” `rti_common.c` L10–L85 has a static RTI context, external start time, locks and initialized scheduling nodes; L342–L392 updates NET and dispatches through callbacks. Socket handling is in the remote binding; common code is not a standalone program. Smallest offline harness: STANDALONE_RTI build, pinned tag helpers and platform mutex/logging support, one initialized context with connected nodes and static upstream/downstream edges, start time, notification stubs recording TAG/PTAG/DNET, serialized NET/LTC and forwarding metadata events, and assertions on outputs. Board service additionally needs register/data transport, reset/epochs, admission ordering, single serialization authority and live grant dispatch. **Replacement:** keep M-0 oracle separate, verify board binding at G-A1 and block M-A2 if unavailable. Feasible in principle on Linux, not built in this review.

**Medium — Two blocks are a wrapper change, not an existing port feature.** Original AS-3 offered a mixed fallback for M-A2. Chisel [`src/main/scala/Main.scala` L42–L53](https://github.com/erlingrj/reactor-chisel/blob/d94dd2d272ce46145ac13c3c6a141bc2ba9dfca1/src/main/scala/Main.scala#L42-L53) elaborates one accelerator factory; `CodesignTopReactor.scala` L37–L43 and L126–L173 define per-instance control and memory I/O. Two distinct modules can be proposed under a parent, but need independent register windows, commands and IRQ/trace identity, memory/DMA arbitration, isolated reset and ordered data paths. No proof of two-instance resource fit or board wrapper compatibility. **Replacement:** simulate independence in M-A1 and require two hardware participants at M-A2; mixed smoke test is not an exit.

**Medium — Gates and acceptance evidence.** Original “Gates are defined here, before they are used” followed earlier references. Move the register before Summary. A2 now needs an absence case whose grant trace includes both closure predicates and an intentionally stalled transfer. G-A3 requires board-binding evidence in addition to M-2. B1/B2 remain independent; only B-HW1 joins the A and B prerequisites. No circular milestone dependency identified after the correction; report thresholds for later campaigns remain to be set from workloads.

**Arithmetic reproducer for DN-5 / HS-3.** Use elapsed `now = 2^32 + 1000 ns` and a next-event low word `2000` in the same high-word epoch. Both low words are positive signed-32 values, so the low-word guards do not explain the error. Correct next time is `4294969296 ns`; the pinned expression gives `2001 ns`. Expected relative delay is `1000 ns`, actual subtraction gives `-4294966295 ns`. This proves an arithmetic error in the reconstruction for these inputs, not that the port reaches it with its supported time range. Fix must specify wrapping and epoch contract rather than only multiply the upper word.
